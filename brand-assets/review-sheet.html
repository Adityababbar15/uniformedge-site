The idea

A geometric U — the first letter of the name — cut by a 45° chamfer, the way a pressed crease catches light down a trouser leg. The cut is the edge.

Why it works

Most ownable of the three. The chamfer is a device that can repeat across the brand: page corners in the catalogue, the corner of a woven label, a section divider.

Watch out for

The brass sliver is the smallest element in the set. Below about 14 px it reads as a colour flick rather than a cut — acceptable, but it means the favicon leans on the U, not the crease.

The idea

Two lapel planes falling away from a tailored notch, deliberately asymmetric — the way a placket overlaps rather than meeting flush.

Why it works

Fastest to read as garment at a glance, and the strongest shape at small sizes. Embroiders in two flat fills with no thin strokes at all.

Watch out for

The nearest to a shape other brands already use — a chevron is common. The asymmetry and the notch gap are what keep it distinct, so neither can be tidied up later without losing the idea.

The idea

A bold geometric E for Edge, with a tailored notch cut into the centre arm — one detail doing all the work.

Why it works

The clearest link between mark and name, and the most institutional of the three. Holds up at 16 px better than either alternative.

Watch out for

A single letter is a weaker standalone asset than a shape: on a garment tag with no wordmark beside it, an E alone may not recall the brand.

Clear space

  • Keep clear space equal to the height of the icon on all four sides.
  • On the catalogue cover, double it — the mark should not compete with the cover image.
  • Nothing enters that space: no rules, no page numbers, no school crests.

Don'ts

  • Don't recolour the accent per school. One brass, everywhere.
  • Don't outline, emboss, or add a drop shadow for embroidery — send the digitiser flat vectors.
  • Don't stretch the stacked version to fill a footer; use the horizontal one.
  • Don't set the wordmark in a substitute font when the logo file is inconvenient.

Distinctness

None of the three uses a graduation cap, crest, needle, or child silhouette. All three are geometric rather than script-based, which keeps clear distance from “The Edge” in Bengaluru — a wordmark-only, no-icon brand. Worth a formal trademark search before final files.

Still open

  • Concept choice, and any cross-pollination between the three.
  • Wordmark typeface. Shown in Avenir Next; the release files need a licensed face, outlined.
  • Dates and budget — section 11 of the brief is still blank.
